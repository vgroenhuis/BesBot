BesBot

Drive with the pad (touch or mouse), the keyboard (arrow keys or W A S D, space to stop) or a gamepad (left stick; B stops; Y switches the drive mode; in lab mode the D-pad does the steps). The robot stops by itself when the commands stop coming.

Watching

One person controls the robot at a time; everyone can watch. To drive, ask for control and type the code the robot shows. Stop works for everyone.

Controller terms

Top: the model’s acceleration, P = Kp · (target − position) (the spring) plus D = −2ζ√Kp · speed (the damper); there is no I term. The sum is held to ±2 m/s² (grey lines). Bottom: the motor power that makes the wheel follow the model: feed-forward (from the calibration: dead zone + speed + acceleration), P on the speed error, I on the position error (the integral of the speed error), and their sum, at most ±100 %.

Each wheel follows a mass-spring-damper towards its target: acceleration = Kp · (target − position) − 2ζ√Kp · speed. Natural frequency √Kp/2π (Kp 39.5: 1 Hz). ζ = 0 undamped (it keeps swinging), below 1 underdamped (overshoot), 1 critically damped, above 1 overdamped (slow). Clicks add up; STOP stops it. The robot can't go faster than about 0.6 m/s or accelerate faster than 2 m/s²: where the model asks for more it is held to that (“limited”) and stops being linear. Undamped swings then stay smaller than the step, but a high Kp brakes too late: with Kp above about 250 a 0.5 m step overshoots even at ζ = 1. Smaller steps keep it linear longer.

Settings

Acceleration: how much the speed may change per 0.1 s (100 %: at once). Minimum power and trim: power mode only. Saved on the robot.

Drive mode and wheel calibration
Drive mode

The calibration takes about 35 s. The robot spins in place, both ways, up to full speed: put it on the floor with half a metre free around it (or lift it, but the floor gives values that fit driving better). STOP ends it.

The speed loop keeps each wheel at the speed it is asked for (in Speed mode, and in Lab mode where the model asks): on top of the feed-forward from the table it adds P (extra power per speed error) and I (extra power per mm the wheel has fallen behind). The calibration sets both, for a response in about 0.12 s without overshoot. Not the same as the lab model's Kp, which sets the spring.
